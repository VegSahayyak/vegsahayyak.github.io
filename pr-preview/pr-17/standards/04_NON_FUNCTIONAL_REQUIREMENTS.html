Non-Functional Requirements (NFRs) ​

Because Speed Assist is a continuous-tracking app targeting safety and potentially fleets, Non-Functional Requirements are just as critical as the features themselves.

1. Battery & Resource Optimization ​

Continuous GPS polling is the #1 reason users uninstall driving apps.

  • Adaptive Polling: Do not request high-accuracy 1-second GPS updates if the user is stationary for > 5 minutes. Downscale the request interval dynamically.
  • Wakelocks: Use ForegroundService properly with clear notifications rather than raw CPU Wakelocks whenever possible. Release resources in onDestroy() or onStop().
  • Auto-Start Efficiency: Leverage :speed-autostart (Google Play Services IN_VEHICLE transition) for zero-friction background trip detection instead of continuous CPU wakelocks, saving massive battery power while waiting for trips to start.

2. Location & Privacy Compliance (Crucial) ​

  • Permissions: Always handle permission degradation gracefully. If ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION is denied but foreground is granted, the app should still function while open.
  • Dialogs: Adhere strictly to Google Play policies by showing explicit "Prominent Disclosure" rationale dialogs before requesting background location. (See dialogs.showLocationPermissionRationale).

3. Performance & Stability ​

  • Compose Recomposition: Avoid passing unstable data classes or heavily mutating lists directly to Compose functions. Use remember and key blocks. Monitor for "jank" caused by excessive recomposition in HUD modes.
  • GPS Jitter (EMA & Schmitt Trigger): Raw GPS data is noisy at low speeds. The app implements Exponential Moving Average (EMA $\alpha = 0.3$) smoothing and Dual Schmitt Trigger Hysteresis ($+1.0\text{ km/h} / -3.0\text{ km/h}$) to eliminate velocity jitter and prevent alarm fluttering at boundary limits.

4. Background Resiliency ​

  • OS Kill Prevention: Android (Doze mode) aggressively kills background apps. Always utilize a highly optimized ForegroundService with a persistent, non-dismissible notification to prevent the OS from silently killing the app during a drive.

5. Safety & Distraction Optimization (Legal NFR) ​

  • Voice & Audio First: The UI must not distract the driver. Once driving begins, the screen should require zero interaction. Rely on audio escalation and high-contrast, glanceable UIs (HUD mode).
  • Drive Mode Lockout: Consider disabling complex UI interactions or typing while the vehicle is in motion to comply with hands-free and distraction laws.
  • HUD Mode: The HudDisplay component enforces distraction safety by utilizing a high-contrast, pure OLED black (#000000) background intended strictly for windshield reflection during night driving, eliminating peripheral glare.

6. Hardware Heterogeneity Handling ​

  • Calibration Offset: Different phones have varying GPS chip qualities, and car speedometers are intentionally calibrated to show faster speeds than reality. Allow users to manually adjust the app's speed calculation by a percentage offset to match their dashboard perfectly.

7. App Size & Offline Capability ​

  • Zero Cloud Reliance: The core speed tracking and alerting must function 100% offline without an internet connection. Do not rely on cloud processing for real-time safety critical alerts.
  • Lightweight Footprint: Total download size and runtime memory footprint must both remain $< 5\text{ MB}$.

8. SDK Modular Architecture (B2B Ecosystem) ​

Per the research failure analysis, the consumer app market is tough, and B2B SDK licensing is the future.

  • Core Independence: The location tracking and speed calculation logic (TelematicsEngine, SpeedWatcher, ISpeedRepository) in :speed-core is completely decoupled with zero UI or ad dependencies.
  • Modular Mindset: Features are isolated across :speed-core, :speed-ui, :speed-overlay, :speed-autostart, and :speed-bom with minimal public API surface.

9. App Startup & Launch Weight ​

  • Lightweight Initialization: The app must open instantly. Do not initialize heavy SDKs, database migrations, or complex location requests synchronously on the main thread during Application.onCreate() or Activity.onCreate(). Defer non-essential initializations until after the first frame has rendered.
